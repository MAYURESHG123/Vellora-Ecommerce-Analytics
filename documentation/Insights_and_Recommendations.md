# Insights & Recommendations

## Repeat Purchasing Is Extremely Common

Out of 4,000 customers with at least one qualifying order, 3,995 placed two or more orders. That gives a repeat customer rate of **99.88%**. Repeat customers generated **99.98%** of order sales.

This makes repeat purchasing a major part of the observed revenue base. It also means the simple two-order definition does not separate loyalty very well in this dataset. A useful next step would be to define loyalty using recency, frequency, monetary value, or customer lifetime value.

## The First 60 Days Are an Important Retention Window

The median time to a second purchase is **44 days**, and **60.9%** of repeat customers made their second purchase within 60 days.

A useful follow-up would be to examine the first 30, 60, and 90 days and identify which products, channels, discounts, or segments are associated with faster second purchases.

## Discount Users Show a Higher Repeat Rate

Discount users had a **99.97%** repeat rate compared with **98.04%** among non-discount users, a difference of **1.93 percentage points**.

This is an association, not proof that discounts caused customers to return. Customers who are already more engaged could be more likely to receive or use discounts.

A stronger follow-up would control for customer characteristics and examine discount depth, acquisition source, and repeat behaviour over time.

## Annual Plans Generate Much More Revenue in This Dataset

Annual plans generated approximately **$19.84M**, compared with **$1.93M** from monthly plans. Annual-plan AOV was **$1,212.88**, compared with **$116.72** for monthly plans.

This shows substantially larger annual transactions, but revenue alone does not establish profitability. Margin, churn, acquisition cost, servicing cost, and renewal behaviour would be needed for that conclusion.

## Add-on Adoption Is Strong

The add-on attach rate is **62.26%**, meaning a substantial share of base-product customers also purchased at least one add-on.

Products with high base-product penetration could be analysed alongside their most frequently purchased add-ons to identify potential bundles or cross-sell journeys.

## Refunds Have Multiple Causes

There were **689 refunded orders**, corresponding to a **2.05%** order-level refund rate. The main recorded reasons were billing error, accidental purchase, duplicate order, and service dissatisfaction.

Because these causes are different, they should not necessarily be addressed in the same way. Billing and duplicate-order refunds are operational issues, while service dissatisfaction is more closely related to customer experience.

## Product Reach and Revenue Tell Different Stories

Some products have broad reach among repeat customers but relatively modest revenue, while some higher-value products generate substantial repeat-customer sales.

Looking at both repeat-customer reach and repeat-customer sales gives a more complete view of product performance than using revenue alone.

## Overall Takeaway

The dataset shows very high repeat purchasing, strong annual-plan revenue, meaningful add-on adoption, and a measurable refund rate.

The next level of analysis would be deeper customer segmentation using recency, frequency, monetary value, product mix, and subscription behaviour rather than relying only on a repeat/no-repeat definition.
